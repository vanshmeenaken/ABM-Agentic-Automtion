# Tone Rules — By Channel

Learned through Email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp agent iterations.

---

## Universal Tone Rules (All Channels)

**✓ DO:**
- Conversational but business-focused
- Direct, specific language (no hedging)
- Confidence without pushiness
- Peer-to-peer (not subordinate)

**✗ DON'T:**
- No ALL CAPS except rare emphasis
- No excessive punctuation (!!!???)
- No dashes for pauses (use line breaks instead)
- No hedging ("might", "could", "perhaps", "possibly")
- No apologies or over-politeness
- No emojis (keep professional)

---

## Email-Specific Tone

**Characteristics:**
- Professional, slower pace
- Paragraph-based (mobile readability)
- Threading maintains single conversation

**Tone elements:**
- Opening: Direct with research hook
- Body: Specific finding + competitive context
- CTA: Confident, not tentative
- Close: "Looking forward to connecting" (warm but not over-eager)

**Example opening:**
> "Hi Rajesh, we recently finished a mandate on Crop Protection Pesticides. While reviewing the findings, we discovered something most leaders at your level aren't tracking yet..."

**NOT:**
> "Hi Rajesh, I hope this email finds you well. We've been researching your industry and thought you might be interested..."

---

## LinkedIn-Specific Tone

**Characteristics:**
- Peer-to-peer, casual DM voice
- Short, punchy lines (no dashes, use line breaks)
- One idea per paragraph
- Conversational ("Hi [name], [insight]")

**Tone elements:**
- Opening: Direct pain point or observation
- Body: Research context + competitive gap
- CTA: "Worth [timeframe] to [action]?"
- Follow-up: Casual reference to timing

**Example:**
> "Hi Rajesh, Dealer NPS declining as farm consolidation shifts power.
>
> We're conducting research on how crop protection companies are repositioning. Our research addresses the gap between dealer power consolidation and your current positioning.
>
> Worth a 30-minute call? We walk through how top performers are approaching this differently, and you tell us whether it maps to your [specific challenge] at Syngenta."

**NOT:**
> "Hi Rajesh, I would like to compare notes with you on your dealer strategy. Would you be available for a quick chat?"

---

## WhatsApp-Specific Tone

**Characteristics:**
- Very conversational, direct, urgent
- One message bubble = one idea
- Fast cadence (Day 1, Day 2-3, Day 5-7)
- Mobile-native (short, scannable)

**Tone elements:**
- Opening: Quick insight hook, no preamble
- Body: Single pain point or finding
- CTA: Direct, time-specific ("quick 30-min call")
- Follow-ups: Casual, low-pressure

**Example M1:**
> "Hi Rajesh, quick insight from our research on Crop Protection Pesticides:
>
> Dealer NPS declining as farm consolidation shifts power to bundled-solution majors.
>
> Would you be open to a quick 30-min call to explore? We walk through what we found."

**M1 Follow-up 1:**
> "No pressure — just thought this mapped to Syngenta India."

**M1 Follow-up 2:**
> "Still interested in a quick chat?"

**NOT:**
> "Hi Rajesh, we have some market research findings. Please let me know if you'd like to schedule a call to discuss. Thank you."

---

## Why Tone Matters

**Email** needs professionalism because it's formal channel. Threading creates conversation feel.

**LinkedIn** needs peer voice because it's social. DMs bypass formality — use it.

**WhatsApp** needs urgency and brevity because it's mobile + intimate. One bubble at a time.

**Hedging kills credibility:** "Could be valuable" = unprepared. "We discovered something most leaders aren't tracking yet" = confident research.

**Dashes are awkward on mobile:** WhatsApp line breaks feel natural. Email paragraphs feel natural. LinkedIn short lines feel natural. Match the channel.
